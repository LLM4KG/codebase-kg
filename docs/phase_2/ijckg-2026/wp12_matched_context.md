## The confound

WP5 compared five conditions at a 7,000-token cap. The KG-augmented arm never approached
it — it retrieved 371–1,532 tokens per task — while BM25 and dense filled to 4,602–6,981.
The *realised* context therefore differed by 4–13×, and "BM25 30/30 vs KG 25/30" on Claude
measured retrieval quality **and** budget at once. A reviewer can say so, and would be right.

WP12 re-runs the two baselines capped at exactly the KG's own measured per-task context —
P1 626, P2 1,532, P3 772, P4 371, P5 497, P6 841 tokens — and reports both views. The
budgets are read out of `wp5_outcomes.csv` by a script, not typed, so they cannot drift
from the arm they are matched to.

**This is an ablation, not a sixth and seventh baseline.** Nobody deploys BM25 at 371
tokens. The 7,000-token view remains the realistic-deployment comparison and the headline;
the matched view isolates selection quality from budget. Neither table is quoted without
the other. The whole-file oracle stays **uncapped** in both views — capping it would stop
it being an upper bound (ground rule 2).

## What it found

Passed out of 30, on the graded scale. No candidate in either leg was quarantined, so
these are also the raw `test_pass` counts.

| Model | Retriever | @7,000 (published) | @matched | KG-augmented (unchanged) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude Sonnet 4.6 | BM25 | 30 | **18** | 25 |
| Claude Sonnet 4.6 | dense | 30 | **15** | 25 |
| Qwen3-Coder | BM25 | 22 | **15** | 16 |
| Qwen3-Coder | dense | 23 | **18** | 16 |

**On Claude the confound accounted for the whole gap and then some.** The KG arm loses to
both baselines at 7,000 tokens (25 vs 30 and 30) and beats both at its own budget
(25 vs 18 and 15). The baselines were not selecting better; they were spending 5–10× more
to cover the same ground.

**On Qwen it is not a sweep, and the note should not pretend otherwise.** The KG arm
edges matched BM25 (16 vs 15) but still trails matched dense (18). Dense at a tight budget
is a real competitor for the open model, and the paper should say so rather than average
the two legs.

### The prediction, and where it was incomplete

Recorded on 20 Sep, before any of these runs: *the KG's relative position improves on P2,
P4 and P6.* All three moved that way on both legs. But **P5/dense on Claude also collapsed,
5 → 0, and was not predicted.** The retention table explains it after the fact — dense drops
`CartProducts.tsx` at P5's 497-token budget, 2/2 → 1/2 — so it is consistent evidence, not
an anomaly. The prediction was directionally right and not exhaustive; both halves of that
belong in the record.

### The counter-case worth a sentence in the paper

Qwen/dense **improved** under the tighter budget on two tasks: P2 4 → 5 and P3 3 → 5. A
smaller, denser context helped the open model where a 7,000-token fill had hurt it. This is
the one result that does not fit "more budget helps, matched budget hurts", and averaging
it away would be the easy mistake.

P2 is the sharper half of this: Claude scored 0/5 on the *same* context Qwen scored 5/5 on.
That is taken apart under [anomalies](#the-two-anomalies-resolved-21-sep), because
the explanation bears on how much a pass-rate can be trusted when context is thin.

## Why the arms move: the retrieval side

`scripts/wp12_matched_retrieval_diag.py` answers this without any generation, Docker or API
spend — dense is a cache-only read and the script exits if `api_calls != 0`. A generation
result only moves when the target file moved:

| Task | Budget | Files to edit | BM25 @7,000 | BM25 @matched | dense @7,000 | dense @matched |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | 626 | 1 | 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1 |
| P2 | 1,532 | 3 | 3/3 | **1/3** | **2/3** | **1/3** |
| P3 | 772 | 1 | 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1 | 1/1 |
| P4 | 371 | 3 | 3/3 | **2/3** | 3/3 | **2/3** |
| P5 | 497 | 2 | 2/2 | 2/2 | 2/2 | **1/2** |
| P6 | 841 | 1 | 1/1 | **0/1** | 1/1 | 1/1 |

P3's `useCopyToClipboard.ts` is *created* by the task, so no retriever can return it at any
budget; it is excluded from the counts rather than scored as a miss.

Two different mechanisms hide behind "dropped", and the paper should keep them apart:

- **Ranking failure.** P6: `CategoryList.tsx` is 669 tokens against an 841 budget, so it
  *fits* — BM25 simply put five other files ahead of it. The 7,000-token cap was hiding a
  ranking error, not compensating for a tight budget.
- **Budget pressure.** P4: dense ranks `useCartProducts.ts` **second** and still drops it,
  because at 371 tokens the fill runs out before the ranking does. Here the cap is doing
  the damage, and no improvement in ranking would help.

### Retention and the outcome, on Claude — *corrected 21 Sep*

Measured against the reference patch's `files_modified`, retention *is* the outcome in
**11 of the 12** Claude matched cells: every target retained → 5/5 passed, any target dropped
→ 0/5, with BM25 on P6 the single exception.

**That metric was wrong, and the corrected figure is 8 of 12.** Retention scored against
`files_modified` mis-measures adequacy wherever a task admits more than one solution layer.
Scored instead as *does the context contain some witnessed passing solution?* it flips five of
the 24 cells, all in the same direction. Working:
[`wp12_anomalies.md`](wp12_anomalies.md), tables [`wp12_apply_loss.md`](wp12_apply_loss.md).

| | correspondence | exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| published retention (`files_modified`) | 11 / 12 | P6/BM25 |
| corrected retention (witnessed sufficiency) | **8 / 12** | P4/BM25, P4/dense, P2/dense, P6/BM25 |

Where the correspondence holds it is tight for the right reason: the failures are
`search_not_found` and `file_not_found` — the model attempting to edit a file it was never
shown — so the retrieval-side diagnosis and the generation-side failure mode name the same
event. Where it does not hold, the context carried a complete and demonstrably sufficient file
set and the model still failed. **Losing the target file reliably costs the cell; keeping it
does not reliably win it** — which is a weaker claim than this section originally made, and the
one the data supports.

On Qwen the correspondence is much weaker, which is itself a finding.

## What the comparison supports

The whole-file oracle is what disciplines the reading. On Claude it passes **30/30** using
278–2,611 tokens — at or *below* the KG's own budget on four of six tasks (P1, P3, P5, P6;
it is above on P2 and P4). So at comparable budget:

| Localisation quality, at comparable budget | Claude | Qwen |
|---|---|---|
| whole-file oracle (perfect localisation, uncapped) | 30/30 | 17/30 |
| KG-augmented | 25/30 | 16/30 |
| BM25 @matched | 18/30 | 15/30 |
| dense @matched | 15/30 | 18/30 |
| floor (no context) | 0/30 | 0/30 |

Three things follow, and only three.

1. **On Claude the WP5 ordering was an artefact of budget.** Nothing about the retrievers
   changed between the two views except how many tokens they were allowed, so the baselines'
   apparent advantage was bought with 5–10× the context rather than with better selection.
2. **Small context is not the problem; wrong context is.** The oracle wins outright at
   KG-sized budgets, and the floor gets nothing at any budget. At comparable budget the
   outcome tracks localisation quality monotonically on Claude.
3. **The KG is bounded from above by its own localisation, not by its budget.** The 5-point
   gap to the oracle is entirely P4 — a task the KG arm fails at 371 tokens while the oracle
   passes at 599. That is the KG's own miss, and the matched ablation is what makes it
   attributable.

On Qwen the ladder collapses — the oracle is not even top — which says the generator, not
the retrieval, is the binding constraint there. **Retrieval quality only pays off when the
model is strong enough to exploit it**, and that is worth stating in the paper as a
condition on the contribution rather than discovered in review.

## What the comparison does not support

Three limits, in decreasing order of how likely a reviewer is to raise them.

**1. Token-matching is not information-matching.** 371 tokens of KG context is structured
facts across several components; 371 tokens of BM25 context is roughly three raw files. The
ablation removes the budget confound and leaves a **representation** confound untouched, and
that one cuts against the baselines. So the two views *bracket* rather than resolve:
**@7,000 is an upper bound on baseline performance and @matched is a lower bound.** Reporting
both is not diplomacy, it is the actual claim. The experiment that would separate selection
from compression — BM25's *choice of files* rendered through the KG's Format A — is not in
this leg and belongs in future work.

**2. "The KG selects better" is not a general claim.** On Qwen the matched ordering is
dense 18, KG 16, BM25 15. Dense wins. At n=5 per cell those three are inside noise, so the
defensible statement is that the matched-budget ordering is **model-dependent**, and the
Claude result is one leg rather than a finding. The two legs should not be averaged.

**3. Matched budget does not equalise the classifier.** The KG arm spends an LLM call to
localise; BM25 and dense spend none. Equalising tokens does not equalise that, and WP9 costs
it as the KG's per-task overhead. A reviewer may fairly ask what BM25 would do with an
LLM query-rewriting step in front of it; this leg does not answer that.

## The two anomalies, resolved *(21 Sep)*

Both are closed. Full working, measurements and the corrections they forced:
[`wp12_anomalies.md`](wp12_anomalies.md). In short:

**P2, dense @matched — identical context, opposite outcome. This was the applier.** Both legs
saw the same 1,497-token context; Claude scored 0/5 and Qwen 5/5. `docker/apply_edits.js` is
all-or-nothing, and Claude attempted the full reference-shaped fix — two edits on the retrieved
`note.ts`, two on the unretrieved `NoteList.tsx` — so one bad SEARCH discarded the lot. Both
`note.ts` edits would have applied in **all five** Claude candidates, which is exactly the pair
Qwen passed with. Replayed per-edit through the unmodified harness, the cell is 5/5.

The consequence is narrower than it first looked. WP12's matched totals were published as
upper bounds on the damage a thin context did; measured, **three of the four do not move at
all** (Claude BM25 18, Qwen 15 and 18). Only Claude dense @matched moves, 15 → 20, and only on
P2. That one figure carries a sentence of qualification in the paper; the other three carry
none. The counterfactual is never added to a pass rate —
[`wp12_per_edit_replay.md`](wp12_per_edit_replay.md) opens with that in a box.

**P6, BM25 @matched — target retained 0/1, yet 3/5 passed. This was the metric.** An earlier
draft of this note said six of ten candidates repaired `CategoryList.tsx` without being shown
it. **That was false.** All ten edited `src/client/slices/category.ts`, a file BM25 *did*
retrieve, at the reducer layer — which `P6.yaml` blesses as valid. Nobody edited
`containers/CategoryList.tsx`. One candidate reached for the component and invented
`components/AppSidebar/CategoryList.tsx`, a path that does not exist, and died on
`file_not_found` — evidence of *no* source-level knowledge of it. The correction to the
retention metric is folded into the section above.

## The code change underneath

Dispatch now keys on the condition TOML's `retriever` field rather than the condition id
(`src/generation/orchestrator.py`), and WP8's `KG_CONDITIONS` name tuple is deleted. A
condition is a *configuration* of a retriever — `kg_augmented_hardened` and `bm25_matched`
differ from their parents only in `[retriever_params]` — so a new condition now needs a TOML
and nothing else, instead of a name tuple per retriever family kept in sync by hand.

A `*_matched` condition missing a task from its `token_budget_by_task` table **raises**
rather than falling back to 7,000. An arm labelled "matched" that had silently run at the
default budget would be WP8's silent-floor defect in a new costume: a condition labelled one
thing and behaving as another, invisible in every artifact except a per-candidate token
count nobody reads.
