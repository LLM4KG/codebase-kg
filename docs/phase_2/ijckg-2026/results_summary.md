## 2. Observations

**The floor is 0/60.** Neither model passed a single task without context, at any budget,
on either leg. Context is necessary; that is the one result in this study with no
qualification attached to it.

**On Claude, three conditions sit at the ceiling.** Whole-file, BM25 and dense all score
30/30 at 7,000 tokens. A ceiling is not a tie: it means **this task set cannot separate
retrieval strategies at a generous budget with a strong model**, so the published view
discriminates only where a condition falls short of it. This is a limitation of the task
set, and §8.4 states it as one.

**The KG arm's entire deficit on Claude is one task.** Per task, against every other
condition:

| Claude: KG vs … | Total | KG better | KG worse | Equal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| whole-file (oracle, uncapped) | 25 – 30 | — | **P4** | 5 tasks |
| BM25 @7,000 | 25 – 30 | — | **P4** | 5 tasks |
| dense @7,000 | 25 – 30 | — | **P4** | 5 tasks |
| BM25 @matched | 25 – 18 | P2, P6 | — | 4 tasks |
| dense @matched | 25 – 15 | P2, P5 | — | 4 tasks |

So on Claude the KG arm **never loses a task at a matched budget** and loses exactly one
task — the same one — at a generous budget. P4 is the KG's own localisation miss, not a
budget effect: the oracle passes P4 on 599 tokens while the KG arm fails on 371
(`wp12_matched_context.md`). That makes the gap attributable, which is the strongest thing
the matched ablation buys.

**On Qwen there is no clean story, and the paper should not manufacture one.**

| Qwen: KG vs … | Total | KG better | KG worse | Equal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| whole-file (oracle, uncapped) | 16 – 17 | P5, P6 | P2, P3, P4 | 1 task |
| BM25 @7,000 | 16 – 22 | P5 | P2, P3, P4 | 2 tasks |
| dense @7,000 | 16 – 23 | P6 | P2, P4 | 3 tasks |
| BM25 @matched | 16 – 15 | P6 | P3 | 4 tasks |
| dense @matched | 16 – 18 | P5, P6 | P2, P3 | 2 tasks |

The oracle is not even top on Qwen (17/30, below both 7,000-token baselines), which says
the **generator, not the retrieval, is the binding constraint on that leg**. Retrieval
quality only pays off when the model is strong enough to exploit it. State that as a
condition on the contribution rather than let a reviewer discover it.

**Do not average the two legs.** They disagree in direction, not just magnitude: at a
matched budget Claude orders KG > BM25 > dense and Qwen orders dense > KG > BM25. At n=5
per cell those matched orderings are inside noise, so the defensible statement is that
the matched-budget ordering is **model-dependent**.

**Where the failures land differs by model, and that is a finding in itself.** On Claude
every non-pass in the whole study is `never_applied` — 30 floor and 5 KG — with no test
or build failures anywhere. Claude either applies a working edit or emits a SEARCH block
that does not match. Qwen's failures spread across all three stages (§1.1), so its lower
pass rate is partly a formatting-compliance effect and not only a reasoning one.

### 3.3 Deterministic sanity scope

**1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00** over 78 items, and the `package.json` check of `Project`, `Library`
and `DEPENDS_ON` is perfect on all five repos. That is what "expected-perfect" was supposed
to mean, and it is the check that the headline is measuring the LLM and not the file walker.

### 3.4 Three measurements converge on one root cause

This is the synthesis no single artifact states, and it is the most useful thing §3 offers
the paper:

| Measurement | What it found |
|---|---|
| WP7 error distribution | **18 of 20 false positives** and 8 of 17 false negatives fall in `Prop` / `EventHandler` / `ACCEPTS_PROP` / `HAS_HANDLER` |
| WP0 step-7 cold re-extraction | LLM-derived node/edge Jaccard 0.89–0.99 against the committed graphs; **the only types that differ are those same four** |
| WP4b gap G2 | "functions that are neither DOM handlers nor components" — the most frequent schema gap, **5 of 6 tasks and every partially-representable unit** |

All three are the same ambiguity: **what a component owns versus what it passes to a
child.** One prompt (`props_and_handlers`) produces it, one schema gap (G2) explains why
the extractor has nowhere correct to put the item, and the instability across re-runs is
the extractor changing its mind about the same judgement. The remaining error is two
localised resolution bugs, not a family: `ROUTES_TO` R 0.43 (four sibling routes in
SnapShot's `App.js` missed, and one *hallucinated* `path='*'` where the JSX has no `path`
attribute at all) and `USES_CUSTOM_HOOK` R 0.38 (all five misses are Todoist's
`src/context/index.js`, a barrel that only re-exports — §8.9).

**One caveat on the attribution table in [`wp7_extraction_accuracy.md`](wp7_extraction_accuracy.md):
it tags all 37 errors `cause: llm`, and five of them are not.** The five
`USES_CUSTOM_HOOK` misses are a **Stage 4 cross-file resolution** failure — the annotator's own
notes say so ("a barrel file that only re-exports; the hook is defined in …") — and
`annotation_guide.md` gives `cause: resolution` for exactly this case, with a barrel `index.ts`
as its worked example. The tag does not enter precision, recall or F1, so **no score in §3
changes either way**; what changes is the reading. Attributed correctly, the LLM extractor's
recall error is **12 false negatives, not 17**, with 5 belonging to the deterministic import
resolver — which is the split the WP1 record explicitly asked WP7 to make. Prefer that split
in the paper, and treat §3.1's recall as very slightly pessimistic for the LLM and silent
about the resolver.

### 3.5 The biases, which must be stated with the scores

1. **Pre-filled drafts bias recall upward.** Annotators corrected a draft generated from
   the extractor's own output. A missed item had to be noticed unprompted; an extracted one
   only had to be confirmed. The guide's blind step 1 — read the source and list what is
   there *before* looking at the draft — reduces this but does not remove it.
2. **Effectively a single annotator.** A second annotator covered **4 of 28 files**.
   Cohen's κ is 1.00 on three of them and **0.46** on SnapShot `src/App.js`, where the two
   agreed on 17 of 19 items; the marginals there are skewed enough (17 accepts, 2 rejects)
   that two disagreements dominate κ. Both annotators independently listed the *same* four
   missed routes, which an accept/reject κ cannot see. **Report both numbers** — κ alone
   misreads that file.
3. **The sample was steered by what the extractor found.** A type missed everywhere could
   not have steered the stratification.
4. **The no-item stratum is under-sampled** — one file per repo, where 105 of 283 files
   have no LLM-extracted items. The strata are reported separately for this reason.
5. **`Prop` and `EventHandler` carry run-to-run variance as well as error** (§3.4), so
   their scores are noisier than the others at the same counts.

## 4. Schema coverage (WP4b) — R3's first question

R3 asked whether 11 node types and 30 relationships are sufficient, and how that was
determined. The honest answer is that **the counts were a design choice, not a measured
optimum**, and WP4b replaces the assertion with a measurement: for each of the six tasks,
every unit of change the reference patch makes is classed **R** (fully representable in the
schema), **P** (partially) or **N** (not at all).

| **Total** | | **22** | **6** | **4** | 32 units |

**22 of 32 units (69%) are fully representable and 28 of 32 (88%) at least partially.** P5
— the one task whose change is purely component structure — is fully representable. Every
`N` falls outside React's component model. Six tasks is far too few for a percentage to
generalise, so **quote the counts, not the percentage**.

The four named gaps, in the order they matter:

- **G2 — functions that are neither DOM handlers nor components.** The most frequent gap:
  5 of 6 tasks, every `P` unit. Functions a hook returns and functions a component defines
  only to pass down as props. A `Function` node, or a `RETURNS` edge from `Custom_Hook`,
  would close it. Also the root cause of §3.4.
- **G1 — Redux state and actions.** The gap that *decides a task's outcome*: P2. There are
  no nodes for slices, reducers, actions or selectors, so the KG knows a component talks to
  the store but not what it reads or dispatches. Designed and costed in
  [`wp11_redux_schema_plan.md`](wp11_redux_schema_plan.md) and deliberately deferred (§8.7).
- **G3 — non-React modules** (P2, P3): utilities and enum constants. Files are modelled;
  symbols inside them are not.
- **G4 — styled-components** (P4): styled wrappers are not components, so a component
  rendered through one has no `USES_COMPONENT` edge.

**Cross-check with WP7, now that annotation is scored.** WP4b left this open. The result:
**no false negative was tagged `cause: schema`**, so nothing an annotator found in 28 files
fell outside the existing 11/30. That is a genuine, if narrow, independent check on
sufficiency — and it sharpens the finding rather than just confirming it. G2 and G4 do not
show up in WP7 as *missing* items; they show up as **precision loss**, the extractor
inventing `Prop` and `EventHandler` nodes for styled-component props and for functions that
are only passed down. A schema with nowhere correct to put an item does not fall silent; it
puts the item somewhere wrong.

## 5. Anchor extraction and wrong anchors (WP8) — R3's second question

Full note: [`wp8_anchor_extraction.md`](wp8_anchor_extraction.md) §7. Terminology: the
component is the **classifier**, not the "planner".

One LLM call per candidate produces a task type and a list of anchor names; the names are
bound into the Cypher templates as `$anchorNames` and matched by **exact, case-sensitive
equality**. Anchors are a **hard filter**, not a ranking signal — which is what makes their
failure modes worth measuring.

Measured over all 60 logged classifier calls, replayed from the WP5 logs at zero API cost:

- **Task type: 60/60 correct**, both models, all six tasks.
- **8 of 9 distinct anchors resolve to exactly one graph node.** The single miss is
  `useCopyToClipboard` (P3) — the file the task creates, so it cannot be in the graph.
- **No ambiguity occurred** in either repository. The ambiguity rung is exercised by unit
  tests, not by real data (§8.10).
- **The classifier is not deterministic at temperature 0.** Three of twelve cells vary, and
  Qwen hallucinated `anchor_routes: ["/notes/trash"]` on two P2 candidates — TakeNote's
  graph holds only `/` and `/app`. That is a real wrong anchor in the wild, and precisely
  R3's question.

**The failure mode that matters is not ambiguity but non-resolution.** Because the anchor
`MATCH` is non-optional, an anchor the graph does not hold returns **zero rows**, and the KG
condition renders a 23–55 token header-only context. It therefore degrades to **floor**,
which scored 0/30 — not to a weaker KG condition. Five statements across `classifier.py`
and `cypher_templates.md` had documented the opposite, promising a project-wide fallback
that only one of the three templates actually had; all five are corrected.

**This did not occur in the reported runs.** All 60 classifier calls produced a resolving
anchor, so the defect is latent, not realised, and no published number is affected. Say
both halves of that: the defect is real, and it did not fire.

It is now fixed behind an **opt-in** condition (`kg_augmented_hardened`,
`[retriever_params] anchor_resolution = "hardened"`) that resolves exact → case-insensitive
→ `difflib` ≥ 0.80, records every resolution in the retrieval metadata, and falls back to a
bounded project overview with an explicit `anchor_fallback` flag when nothing resolves. The
published `kg_augmented` arm is byte-identical to what WP5 ran. Fed deliberately wrong
anchors, the hardened path recovers a misspelled anchor's context exactly and turns the
empty-anchor case from 23–55 silent tokens into a flagged 198–594 token overview — but a
**wrong-but-real** anchor resolves cleanly and cannot be detected from the graph alone,
which is the residual limit to state plainly.

### 6.2 Per task

The KG condition is the **cheapest** retrieval condition per candidate: **$0.0199** with
Claude against $0.0582 (BM25) and $0.0617 (dense); **$0.0023** with Qwen against $0.0058
and $0.0059. The classifier call is included and costs about $0.0024 per task on Claude —
it is a genuine per-task KG overhead and belongs on the KG's side of the comparison, not
hidden. The saving is simply prompt size: 371–1,532 KG tokens against 4,602–6,981 capped
baseline tokens.

Retrieval latency: the KG's Cypher is **2–7 ms**, comparable to BM25's 40–74 ms index+rank
and dense's 16–36 ms cached ranking. The KG's own retrieval time is dominated by its
classifier LLM call (0.9–7.4 s). The generator call dominates everything.

Out of 30, the KG passed 25 (Claude) and 16 (Qwen) against BM25's 30 and 22 and dense's 30

## 7. The context-budget-matched ablation (WP12)

**Report this beside §1, never instead of it, and label it an ablation — not a sixth and
seventh condition.** Nobody deploys BM25 at 371 tokens. The published 7,000-token view is
the realistic-deployment comparison; this view isolates selection quality from budget.
Neither table should be quoted without the other.

The confound: the KG arm retrieves 371–1,532 tokens per task while BM25 and dense fill to
4,602–6,981, so "BM25 30/30 vs KG 25/30" varies retrieval quality **and** budget at once.
WP12 re-runs the two baselines capped at exactly the KG's own measured per-task context
(P1 626, P2 1,532, P3 772, P4 371, P5 497, P6 841 tokens). The KG arm and the oracle were
**not re-run**; the oracle stays uncapped.

and the oracle is not even top.

### 7.4 Two anomalies, resolved — and one of them cost a claim

Full note: [`wp12_anomalies.md`](wp12_anomalies.md). Both were chased to ground, and they
pull in opposite directions.

**Anomaly A — the applier, not the model.** `docker/apply_edits.js` is all-or-nothing: one
failed SEARCH block aborts the whole edit script. That is correct for the harness but it
collapses "three of four edits were right" into `never_applied`, so WP12's matched totals
were published as *upper bounds on the damage*. All 52 corpus candidates with at least one
acceptable edit were rebuilt with only those edits and re-run through the **unmodified**
applier, harness and images. 9 of 52 would have passed:

| Model | Arm | Published /30 | Counterfactual /30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claude | `bm25_matched` | 18 | 18 |
| Claude | `text_emb_3_large_matched` | 15 | **20** |
| Qwen | `bm25_matched` | 15 | 15 |
| Qwen | `text_emb_3_large_matched` | 18 | 18 |

**Three of the four matched totals do not move** — for those, "upper bound on the damage"
and "measurement of the damage" are the same number. Only Claude dense @matched moves, and
only on P2. So the caveat narrows from all four totals to one cell. Two guards on reading
it: the **floor arm does not move** (0/30 on both legs under per-edit application too, so
WP5's most load-bearing number is not an applier artifact), and it is **not a
baselines-only effect** (on Qwen the oracle gains 2, the KG arm 1, BM25 @7,000 1).

For the paper: WP12's matched totals stand as published; the Claude dense-matched figure
carries one sentence of qualification and the other three carry none. The counterfactual is
never quoted alone and never added to a pass rate.

**Anomaly B — the metric, not the harness, and it weakened a claim.** Retention measured
against the reference patch's `files_modified` mis-measures adequacy wherever a task admits
more than one solution layer — P6's candidates fixed the *reducer* (`slices/category.ts`,
which BM25 did retrieve), a solution `P6.yaml` explicitly blesses. Corrected to ask "does
the retrieved set contain some witnessed passing solution?", five of 24 cells flip, all in
the same direction.

| | correspondence | exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| published retention | 11 / 12 | P6/BM25 |
| **corrected retention** | **8 / 12** | P4/BM25, P4/dense, P2/dense, P6/BM25 |

WP12 had called this "the tightest evidence in this work package" at 11 of 12. It is 8 of
12. **Use the weaker sentence, because it is the true one:** *at a matched budget, losing
the target file reliably costs the cell, but keeping it does not reliably win it.* The
three durable exceptions all say the same thing — P4's matched cells carried the complete
`Cart.tsx` and `useCart.ts`, verbatim, the exact pair a passing Qwen candidate used at
7,000 tokens, and Claude still scored 0/5 with the replay confirming `test_fail` rather
than an applier artifact. The files were there; the models did not use them.

Two further cautions for anyone quoting §7.4. Witnessed sufficiency is **survivorship-biased
by construction** — a solution counts as witnessed only because some arm retrieved enough
to find it — so it is a **lower bound on adequacy**, and `sufficient = False` means "no
*known* solution fits", never "no solution exists". And a passage in
`wp12_matched_context.md` was simply **false** ("six of ten candidates repaired a file they
were never shown"); it is corrected in place, and the corrected version is what to cite.

**Nothing published changed.** WP5 and WP12 regenerate byte-identically, and the applier,
the harness and every condition TOML are untouched.

## 8. Planned but not done, with reasons

Everything in this section is a limitation to write down, not a gap to hide. The first
three are required by the plan; the rest are the ones a reviewer is most likely to find.

**8.1 Only five of the six designed conditions were run.** Nomic Embed Code
(`conditions/base/nomic_embed_code.toml`) has **no retriever implementation** — the config
exists, nothing dispatches to it, and it is marked "not run" in the file itself. The leg
therefore reports five of six. **The paper must not imply the six-condition comparison is
complete.** The dense baseline that *was* run uses OpenAI `text-embedding-3-large` in both
legs, so the open-weight leg is not embedding-model-pure either — deliberately, since the
retriever is the variable under test, but it needs saying.

**8.2 There is no cross-framework evidence at all.** No Angular, Rails or other
non-React target was extracted or evaluated. No experiment was possible in this window, so
**the adaptability claim has to be softened in the writing leg** rather than supported.

**8.3 This remains a pilot, not the controlled study R1 asked for.** Six tasks, two
repositories, n=5 per cell at temperature 0, no confidence intervals, no pass@k, no
paired-bootstrap significance testing. The five candidates in a cell are near-identical by
construction, so they are not independent retries and the counts are not a sample from
which to estimate variance. Every ordering in §1 and §7 should be read as a pilot
observation.

**8.4 The task set saturates at 7,000 tokens on Claude.** Whole-file, BM25 and dense all
reach 30/30. Only the matched view in §7 separates the conditions on that leg, and the one
discriminating signal in the published view is the KG arm's P4 miss. Harder or
longer-horizon tasks are needed before a generous-budget comparison can say anything.

**8.5 Generation tasks exist on only two of the five extracted repositories.**
react-shopping-cart and TakeNote have Docker images; SnapShot, Todoist and Jira Clone do
not, so they contribute to §3 (accuracy) and §6 (cost) but to no generation result.

**8.6 Grading is fully automated.** The Docker harness decides pass/fail. There is no human
or blind grading anywhere in this leg, and no qualitative code-quality judgement — a passing
candidate is one whose edit applied, built and passed the task's tests.

**8.7 The Redux/state schema extension (G1) is designed but deliberately not built.**
[`wp11_redux_schema_plan.md`](wp11_redux_schema_plan.md): 3 node types, 6 relationships,
≈ $1 and 5 minutes to re-extract TakeNote, 3.5–4 days of work. It is deferred to the next
implementation leg (29 Sep at the earliest) because it lands *after* WP7 is frozen —
building it now would invalidate §3's figures and the 11/30 counts the paper cites. P2
stays the documented negative case for G1.

**8.8 `kg_augmented_hardened` was not run end to end.** The hardened anchor resolution is
measured on retrieval only, so there is **no pass-rate evidence for it and none is
claimed**. That is deliberate: on the logged anchors — all 60 of which resolved — the
hardened arm reproduces `strict`'s context token for token, so an end-to-end run could
differ only on a candidate whose anchor fails to resolve, and none did. It would measure
classifier nondeterminism at n=5, not hardening
([`wp8_anchor_extraction.md`](wp8_anchor_extraction.md) §8.1).

**8.9 A barrel-resolution bug found by WP7 is reported, not fixed.** The import ladder does
not resolve past Todoist's `src/context/index.js`, costing five `USES_CUSTOM_HOOK` edges
(§3.4). Fixing it would change the committed graphs and invalidate §3, so it is queued for
the next leg. The accuracy figures therefore include a known, localised, understood defect.

**8.10 No name ambiguity occurred in either repository**, so the hardened resolver's
ambiguity rung and the "two `Button`s in different files" case are exercised by unit tests
rather than by data. R3's ambiguity question is answered by design and by construction, not
by observation.

**8.11 Token-matching is not information-matching.** 371 tokens of KG context is structured
facts across several components; 371 tokens of BM25 context is roughly three raw files. §7
removes the budget confound and leaves a **representation** confound untouched — and that
one cuts *against* the baselines. The two views therefore **bracket** rather than resolve:
@7,000 is an upper bound on baseline performance, @matched a lower bound. The experiment
that would separate selection from compression — BM25's *choice of files* rendered through
the KG's Format A — is future work.

**8.12 No LLM query-rewriting baseline.** The KG arm spends an LLM call to localise; BM25
and dense spend none. Matching tokens does not equalise that. A reviewer may fairly ask what
BM25 would do with a rewriting step in front of it, and this leg does not answer it.

**8.13 Two cost figures are upper bounds.** Extraction used no prompt caching although 98%
of its tokens are input, so §6.1 overstates what a caching implementation would pay. And
two OpenRouter providers applied automatic cache discounts to Qwen calls that our logs do
not record, so the Qwen USD is an upper bound — billed was 68% of the computed figure over
18–19 Sep. Discounts would shrink both sides of the Qwen comparison, not only the KG's.

**8.14 `IMPORTS` is specified but never created** by the pipeline. It is excluded from §3
and reported as not implemented, which means the "30 relationships" the paper cites is 30
*designed*, not 30 *populated*.

## 9. The three sentences this leg supports

If the revision needs a compressed claim, these are defensible on the evidence above and
nothing stronger is:

1. **Context is necessary and its *quality* is what matters, not its size.** Zero context
   passes nothing (0/60); at comparable budget on the stronger model, outcomes track
   localisation quality monotonically from the oracle (30/30) through the KG (25/30) to
   capped lexical and dense retrieval (18/30, 15/30).
2. **A KG-augmented context is competitive at a fraction of the tokens, and that is a cost
   result before it is a quality result.** It is the cheapest condition per task
   ($0.0199 vs $0.0582 / $0.0617 on Claude) and on Claude it loses exactly one task to any
   other condition — but it did not have the highest pass rate at a generous budget, and
   the construction cost takes 158–191 tasks to amortise.
3. **Both of those are conditional on the generator.** On the open-weight leg the ladder
   collapses, the oracle is not top, and the matched-budget ordering reverses. Retrieval
   quality pays off only when the model is strong enough to exploit it.
